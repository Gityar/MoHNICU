Introduction to Newborn Health

Understanding neonatal morbidity and mortality globally and nationally

🎯 Learning Objectives

Chapter 1

By the end of this chapter, participants will be able to:

  • Describe the neonatal morbidity and mortality situation globally
  • Describe the neonatal morbidity and mortality situation nationally

📊 Global Statistics (2021)

  • 2.3 million children died in the first month of life
  • 6,400 newborn deaths every day
  • Decline from 5 million (1990) to 2.3 million (2021)
  • Neonatal deaths = 47% of all under-5 child deaths

🌍 Sub-Saharan Africa

  • Highest neonatal mortality rate globally
  • 27 deaths per 1,000 live births (2019)
  • 10x more likely to die in first month than high-income countries

🇪🇹 Ethiopia Situation

  • Under-5 mortality declined by 71% (1990-2019): 204 → 59 per 1,000
  • Reduction primarily among post-neonatal children
  • Neonatal mortality contribution increased from 43% (1990) to 48% (2021)
  • >80% of newborn deaths caused by preventable conditions: Sepsis, Prematurity, Intrapartum-related conditions

💡 MOH Response

Expansion of quality, high-impact neonatal interventions in health centers and hospitals, including:
• Basic newborn care units (newborn corners) at health centers
• NICUs at hospitals

🏥 Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU)

Chapter 1

👶 Who Needs NICU Care?

  • Babies born early (preterm)
  • Babies with problems during delivery
  • Babies who develop problems while in hospital

🏨 Where is NICU Provided?

  • Primary hospitals
  • General hospitals
  • Tertiary hospitals

⚠️ Establishing NICU is Expensive

Facilities should start providing the most possible care including:

  • Providing training
  • Continuous support for newborn health care providers and managers
  • Ensuring basic equipment and supplies are in place
  • Strengthening facility infrastructure and referral system

Working in the NICU

NICU setup, medical ethics, roles, responsibilities, and respectful communication

🎯 Learning Objectives

Chapter 2

By the end of this module, participants will be able to:

  • Describe what the NICU set-up looks like
  • Depict the ethical aspects of working in the NICU
  • Explain the role and responsibility of nurses in the NICU
  • Describe guidelines for respectful communication with parents

🏥 NICU Setup

  • A unit where premature babies and newborns with serious medical & surgical conditions receive specialized care
  • Most admitted babies are critically sick & need supportive care
  • Mothers are usually sick and unable to be with their babies
  • The NICU unit in Ethiopia is limited in size

💉 Frequently Done Procedures

  • NG tubes
  • Monitors
  • Intranasal oxygen or other respiratory support
  • Intravenous lines and IV medications
  • All procedures are disturbing and painful to the newborn

💎 Values and Behaviors in NICU

Chapter 2

❤️ Compassion

  • How care is given through empathy, respect & dignity
  • Central to how people perceive their care

🎓 Competence

  • Understand individual's health & social needs
  • Expertise, clinical and technical knowledge
  • Evidence-based care delivery

💬 Communication

  • Central to successful caring relationships
  • Key to a good workplace
  • Listening is essential

🦁 Courage

  • Do the right thing for those we care for
  • Speak up when we have concerns
  • Personal strength & vision to innovate

🎯 Commitment

  • Cornerstone of what we do
  • Build commitment to improve care

⚖️ Ethical Principles in NICU

Chapter 2

👨‍👩‍👧 Respecting Parental Authority/Autonomy

  • Right of parents to decide the care of their newborn
  • Support and educating parents is the responsibility of NICU staff

💚 Beneficence

  • Do good, promote the best interests of patients
  • Applying the best interests of the infant standard of judgment

🚫 Non-maleficence

  • Obligation not to inflict harm on others
  • Minimizing harm to the newborn

⚖️ Justice

  • Patients in similar situations have access to the same health care

👩‍⚕️ Roles and Responsibilities

Chapter 2

👩‍⚕️ NICU Nurse

  • Backbone of the unit
  • Follow all rules of dressing for infection prevention
  • Collaborate with the team
  • Have patients assigned
  • Take, interpret, and timely act on abnormal findings
  • Practice strict hand hygiene
  • Be able to do neonatal resuscitation
  • Record and document on chart
  • Hand over patient before leaving shift
  • Involve in rounds

👨‍⚕️ Physicians/Health Officers

  • Follow all dressing and infection prevention rules
  • Collaborate with the team
  • Be able to do neonatal resuscitation and procedures
  • Record and document what has been done
  • Hand over patient before leaving shift
  • Make rounds and be available for consultation 24/7

💬 Respectful Communication with Parents

Chapter 2

Guidelines for Communication

  • Create an environment for communication
  • Identify and remove barriers limiting parents' role
  • Communicate at admission, crisis points, periodic reviews, and unstructured opportunities
  • Encourage parents to seek clarification
  • Provide open, truthful communication at all times
  • Provide information as accurately as possible
  • Identify areas of medical uncertainty
  • Use easily understandable language
  • Assess family communication preferences
  • Be pre-emptive and proactive in communication
  • Convene meetings with both parents for important decisions
  • Keep parents informed of special investigations
  • Recognize the need for time to process information
  • Ensure consistency and continuity of communication
  • Practice open, honest, and timely disclosure regarding medical error

Early Childhood Development (ECD)

Brain development, nurturing care, and neurodevelopmental support in the NICU

🧠 Concept and Benefits of ECD

Chapter 3

📅 Early Childhood Period

  • From conception to six years of age
  • Critical for brain development
  • Three stages: conception-3 years, 3-5 years, 5-6 years

🧠 Brain Development

  • 80% of brain growth occurs in first 3 years
  • Brain size doubles in first year
  • Grows to 50% of adult size by age 2
  • Continuous maturation in cognitive, linguistic, executive functions

🌟 Five Components of Nurturing Care

  • Good health
  • Adequate nutrition
  • Safety and security
  • Responsive caregiving
  • Opportunity for early learning/Early stimulation

💰 Why Invest in ECD?

  • Child's right to develop in its best way
  • Brain develops faster in childhood
  • Early problem identification and treatment
  • Make child ready for school
  • Poverty reduction
  • High return during adulthood

🏥 Caring for Baby's Brain in NICU

Chapter 3

⚠️ Stressful Conditions Affecting Neurodevelopment

  • Sensory overload: bright lights, loud noises
  • Invasive procedures
  • Avoiding test and smell stimulation
  • Modifying the NICU environment is crucial to reduce stress

👂 Sense of Hearing

  • For development of auditory and language centres of brain

✋ Sense of Touch

  • For development of sensory area of brain

👁️ Sense of Vision

  • For development of visual area of brain

💡 Key Point

Stimulation is very important, begins in the NICU and continues at home. Understanding the baby's cues and knowing when he/she is ready to be stimulated is essential.

👨‍👩‍👧 Counseling Caregivers

Chapter 3

🤱 Maternal-Newborn Bonding

  • The first hour of life after birth is the ideal time for bonding
  • Health professionals need effective communication with the mother
  • Counsel the caregiver on stimulating the newborn

📋 Counseling Steps: GREET-ASK-OBSERVE-PRAISE-TRY-EXPLAIN-SOLVE

  • GREET the caregiver
  • ASK about the baby
  • OBSERVE the baby
  • PRAISE what the caregiver is doing well
  • Help the caregiver TRY a new stimulation activity
  • EXPLAIN how stimulation helps the baby develop
  • Help caregiver SOLVE PROBLEMS

History and Physical Examination

Newborn assessment, classification, and clinical evaluation

📋 Neonatal History Taking

Chapter 4

📝 History Components

  • Maternal profile
  • Current pregnancy
  • Previous pregnancy
  • Drug history
  • Family history
  • Labor and delivery
  • Presenting complaint

🔍 Physical Examination Preparation

  • Baby should be naked in a thermo-neutral environment
  • Observe for birth defects, breathing, movement, cyanosis, pallor, jaundice

🧰 Equipment Needed

  • Thermometer
  • Measuring board and tape
  • Weighing scale (for babies)
  • Stethoscope
  • Watch
  • Gloves
  • Data collection sheet

💡 Initial Examination Focus

• Baby's response to transition from fetal to extrauterine life
• Any congenital anomalies
• Any sign of infection

📊 APGAR Score

Chapter 4

APGAR Assessment Components

  • A - Appearance (Skin color)
  • P - Pulse (Heart rate)
  • G - Grimace (Reflex irritability)
  • A - Activity (Muscle tone)
  • R - Respiration (Crying, breathing)

📝 Key Examination Points

• General examination
• Vital signs
• Anthropometric measurement
• Color
• Examination of HEENT
• Mammary glands

📏 Classification of the Newborn

Chapter 4

📅 Based on Gestational Age

  • Preterm: Less than 37 completed weeks
  • Term: 37-42 weeks
  • Post-term: More than 42 weeks

⚖️ Based on Birth Weight

  • Macrosomia: ≥4000g
  • Normal weight: 2500-3999g
  • Low birth weight: 1500-2499g
  • Very low birth weight: 1000-1499g
  • Extremely low birth weight: <1000g

📊 Classification by Weight & Gestational Age

  • AGA (Appropriate for Gestational Age): Birth weight between 10-90th percentile
  • LGA (Large for Gestational Age): Birth weight >90th percentile
  • SGA (Small for Gestational Age): Birth weight <10th percentile

📏 Gestational Age Estimation Methods

• First day of last menstrual period
• Ultrasound estimation
• Ballard score

Essential Newborn Care (ENC)

Standardized procedures for all newborns at birth

👶 Components of Essential Newborn Care

Chapter 5

📌 Definition

Essential newborn care is care given to all newborn infants at birth to optimize their chances of survival.

Step 1: Dry and Stimulate

  • Immediately dry the whole body
  • Keep newborn warm by placing on mother's abdomen
  • Stimulate by rubbing the back or flicking soles of feet

Step 2: Evaluate Breathing

  • Check if baby is crying while drying
  • If not crying, see if breathing properly
  • If not breathing/gasping: Call for help
  • Cut cord rapidly and start resuscitation

Step 3: Cord Care

  • Clamping/tying the cord
  • Cutting the cord
  • Check for bleeding/oozing and re-tie if necessary
  • Watch for pus discharge, redness, swelling, fever

Step 4: Keep Warm

  • Skin-to-skin contact on mother's chest
  • Cover baby's body and head with clean cloth
  • If room is cool (<25°C), use blanket

Step 5: Initiate Breastfeeding

  • Within first one hour
  • Reduces risk of postpartum hemorrhage
  • Colostrum has many benefits
  • Provides best possible nutrition

Step 6: Eye Drops/Ointment

  • Wash hands with soap and water
  • Clean eyes with sterile water swabs
  • Give tetracycline eye ointment within 1 hour

Step 7: Chlorohexidine on Cord

  • Apply to cord stump

Step 8: Vitamin K IM

  • 1 mg for GA ≥34 weeks
  • 0.5 mg for premature <34 weeks

Step 9: Identification Bands

  • Place on wrist and ankle
  • Prevents mix-ups in busy delivery rooms

Step 10: Weigh the Newborn

  • When stable and warm

Step 11: Record Observations

  • Record all observations and treatment
  • Use registers/appropriate chart/cards

⚠️ Normal Breathing

• Normal breathing rate: 30-60 breaths per minute
• No chest in-drawing or grunting
• Small babies may have mild chest in-drawing and periodic breathing

Neonatal Resuscitation

Transition to extrauterine life, risk factors, and resuscitation steps

🫁 Transition & Risk Factors

Chapter 6

🔄 Transition to Extrauterine Life

  • Successful transition depends on significant physiologic changes at birth
  • Baby may have difficulty before, during, or after labor
  • Problems that may disrupt normal transition:
    • Breathing problems
    • Poor cardiac function or bradycardia
    • Persistent pulmonary hypertension
    • Brain damage, damage to other organs, or death

⚠️ Signs of Abnormal Transition

  • Depression of respiratory drive
  • Poor muscle tone
  • Cyanosis
  • Bradycardia
  • Poor perfusion
  • Tachypnea

🔴 Why Premature Babies are at Higher Risk

  • Lungs may be deficient in surfactant
  • More difficult to ventilate
  • Problems with temperature regulation

📋 Risk Factors for Resuscitation

Chapter 6

🤰 Maternal Risk Factors (Before Labor)

  • Preeclampsia and eclampsia
  • Previous fetal or neonatal death
  • Maternal infection (HIV, Malaria)
  • Multiple gestation
  • Premature rupture of membranes
  • Diminished fetal activity
  • Post-term gestation
  • No prenatal care
  • Bleeding in 2nd/3rd trimester
  • Maternal diabetes
  • Age <16 or >35 years
  • Anemia

🏥 Risk Factors During Labor

  • Foul smelling amniotic fluid
  • Precipitous labor
  • Unusual vaginal bleeding before delivery
  • Shoulder dystocia
  • Prolonged labor (>24 hours)
  • Prolapsed cord
  • Fetal bradycardia
  • Prolonged rupture of membranes (>18 hours)
  • Forceps or vacuum-assisted delivery
  • Meconium
  • Narcotics administered to mother

🚑 Basic Steps in Resuscitation

Chapter 6

Initial Steps of Resuscitation

  • Preventing heat loss
  • Drying and removing wet linen
  • Position head and clear airway ("sniffing position")
  • Suction mouth first, then nose (M before N)
  • Stimulation of infant to breathe
  • Apply tactile stimulation by gently rubbing back or flicking soles of feet 2-3 times

💡 Evaluation and Decision-Making

Based primarily on:
• Respiration
• Heart rate
• Color

⏱️ Positive Pressure Ventilation (PPV)

  • Ventilation of lungs is the single most effective step in newborn resuscitation
  • If no response with above methods, start bag and mask ventilation within the Golden Minute
  • Indications:
    • No breathing or gasping respiration, Apnea
    • Heart rate less than 100 per minute

Perinatal Asphyxia

Definition, epidemiology, pathophysiology, and management

🧠 Perinatal Asphyxia Overview

Chapter 7

📌 Definition

  • Failure to initiate and sustain breathing at birth
  • An insult to the fetus or newborn due to lack of oxygen (hypoxia) and/or lack of perfusion (ischemia) to various organs

📊 AAP and ACOG Criteria for Diagnosis

  • Arterial cord pH <7.0 and base deficit >12
  • Apgar score of less than 7 at 5 minutes
  • Evidence of altered neurological status (altered level of consciousness, seizures, hypotonia, obtundation)

📈 Epidemiology

  • Second commonest cause of neonatal mortality
  • Preceded by prematurity-related complications
  • Commonest cause of disability in surviving newborns
  • 90% of deaths caused by preterm birth, intrapartum complications, and infections

⏱️ Risk Factors Timing

  • Antepartum events (20%)
  • Intrapartum (70%)
  • Postpartum Asphyxia (10%)

🔬 Organ Manifestations

  • Perinatal/neonatal depression
  • Neonatal encephalopathy
  • Hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE)
  • Hypoxic-ischemic brain injury
  • Renal dysfunction
  • Cardiovascular dysfunction

🌡️ Therapeutic Hypothermia

Chapter 7

❄️ Standard of Care for HIE

  • Standard of care for brain injury control in term infants with HIE
  • Reduces mortality
  • Reduces long-term neurodevelopmental disability at 12-24 months

📋 Indications

  • Infants ≥36 weeks gestation
  • Birth weight >1800g
  • Age less than 6 hours at admission
  • Infant must have 2 or more:
    • APGAR score ≤5 at 10 minutes
    • Cord or arterial pH <7 or base deficit ≥12 within 60 minutes of birth
    • Need resuscitation for first 10 minutes of life

🌡️ Protocol

• Total body/head cooling within six hours
• Target temperature: 33-34°C
• Duration: 72 hours with slow rewarming

📊 Prognosis of HIE

  • Stage I (mild): 98-100% normal neurological outcome, <1% mortality
  • Stage II (moderate): 20-37% die or have abnormal neurodevelopmental outcome
  • Stage III (severe): Death is more likely; survivors have major neurodevelopmental issues

Thermoregulation

Hypothermia, hyperthermia, and temperature management

🌡️ Neonatal Thermoregulation

Chapter 8

📌 Normal Values

  • Normal body temperature: 36.5°C - 37.5°C
  • Hypothermia: Axillary temperature <36.5°C

⚠️ Why Newborns are at Risk

  • Large surface area for small body mass
  • Premature and LBW babies more at risk
  • Immature skin
  • Deficient subcutaneous and brown fat stores
  • Immature central thermoregulation
  • Poor caloric intake
  • High oxygen consumption due to pulmonary problems

🔥 Mechanisms of Heat Loss

  • Convection: Heat lost from skin to moving air
  • Radiation: Heat dissipated to colder objects in surroundings
  • Conduction: Baby loses heat to surface lying on
  • Evaporation: Major cause immediately after birth; water evaporates from wet skin

📊 Classification of Hypothermia

  • Mild (cold stress): 36°C - 36.4°C
  • Moderate: 32°C - 35.9°C
  • Severe (neonatal cold injury): <32°C

🛡️ Prevention & Management

Chapter 8

Before Delivery

  • Warm delivery room (25-28°C)
  • Organize newborn corner with adequate heat source

During Delivery

  • Deliver baby on mother's abdomen
  • Dry baby thoroughly immediately after birth
  • Remove wet clothes
  • Use cap to prevent heat loss through scalp
  • Plastic wrap for newborns <32 weeks during stabilization

Subsequent Care

  • Arrange appropriate transportation
  • Postpone bathing (after 24 hours)
  • Warm hands and stethoscope
  • Examine/resuscitate under radiant warmer
  • Practice rooming-in
  • Keep newborn away from windows and drafts
  • Continue breastfeeding

💊 Management of Hypothermia

  • Identify and treat causes
  • Put infant on KMC, incubators, or radiant warmer
  • Warm newborn slowly
  • Monitor axillary temperature every hour until stabilized
  • Monitor correct environmental temperature

⚠️ Dangers of Warmers

• Hyperthermia
• Dehydration
• Masks serious infections

Kangaroo Mother Care (KMC)

Skin-to-skin contact for preterm and low birth weight babies

🦘 KMC Definition & Benefits

Chapter 9

📌 Definition

KMC is the early, prolonged (8-24hrs), continuous skin-to-skin contact between the mother (or substitute) and her LBW/preterm babies, at facility and community, with support for correct positioning and exclusive breastfeeding.

👶 For Preterm/LBW Babies

  • Thermal synchrony
  • Enhanced infant sleep patterns
  • Infection prevention
  • Improved brain maturation

👩 For the Mother

  • Promotes mother's confidence
  • Enhances satisfaction
  • Improves bonding
  • Reduces depressive symptoms

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 For Father & Families

  • Involved in protecting and nurturing infants
  • Increases bonding and attachment
  • Increases caregiver's confidence

🏥 For Healthcare Facilities

  • Lower implementation costs
  • Decrease in re-admission rates
  • Decrease in length of stay

🦘 KMC Practice

Chapter 9

📍 KMC Position

  • Place baby between mother's breasts in upright position
  • Turn head to the side, slightly extended
  • Keep airway open
  • Allow eye-to-eye contact
  • Avoid forward flexion & hyperextension of neck
  • Infant should be in flexed position (legs & arms)
  • Secure baby with binder/wrap
  • Top of binder should be at baby's ear

👕 Clothing

  • Mother: Front-open, light dress; suitable wrapper/binder
  • Infant: Hat, socks, and diaper only

⏱️ Duration

  • 8-24 hours per day
  • Supported by mothers, fathers, or surrogates

📋 Monitoring While in KMC

• Airway is clear
• Breathing is regular
• Color is pink
• Infant maintains normal temperature

🚫 When to Discontinue KMC

  • When gestation reaches 40 weeks or weight ≥2,500g
  • Infant starts wriggling uncomfortably
  • Pulls out limbs from kangaroo garment
  • Cries and fusses when mother tries to put infant back

Prematurity

Causes, common problems, and management of preterm infants

👶 Prematurity Overview

Chapter 10

📌 Definition

  • A newborn delivered before 37 completed weeks
  • Have many physiologic challenges adapting to extrauterine life
  • Higher morbidity and mortality compared to term newborns
  • Preterm delivery accounts for 75-80% of newborn morbidity and mortality

🔍 Causes

  • Low socioeconomic status
  • Acute or chronic maternal illnesses
  • Multiple pregnancy
  • Maternal age <20 or >35
  • Obstetric factors: Hypertensive disorders, Antepartal hemorrhage, Cervical incompetence, Uterine anomalies
  • Maternal physical stress
  • Trauma

⚠️ Common Problems of Prematurity

  • Respiratory: RDS, Apnea of prematurity
  • Neurologic: Respiratory center depression, Intra cranial hemorrhage
  • Cardiovascular: Hypotension, Patent ductus arteriosus (PDA)
  • Hematologic: Anemia, Hyperbilirubinemia
  • Nutritional/GI: Feeding problems, NEC
  • Metabolic: Hypo/hyperglycemia, Fluid and electrolyte imbalance
  • Renal: Low GFR, inability to handle water/solute loads
  • Temperature regulation: Immature
  • Immunologic: Immature immune defenses
  • Ophthalmologic: Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP)

🫁 Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS)

Chapter 10

📌 Introduction

  • Primarily caused by immaturity of the lung (lack of adequate surfactant)
  • Surfactant prevents collapse of alveoli at end of expiration

📊 Incidence by Gestational Age

  • <28 weeks: 60-80%
  • 32-36 weeks: 15-35%
  • >37 weeks: 5%
  • Peak severity at 1-3 days

⚠️ Risk Factors

  • Low gestational age
  • Low birth weight
  • Male predominance
  • Maternal diabetes
  • Perinatal asphyxia
  • Elective caesarean section

🩺 Clinical Manifestations

  • Respiratory distress (grunting, flaring, retraction, tachypnea)
  • Markedly decreased air entry bilaterally
  • Cyanosis
  • Low oxygen saturation

💊 Management

  • Maintain normal temperature
  • Assess need for resuscitation
  • Positive pressure ventilation
  • Delivery room CPAP for GA <32 weeks
  • Nasal CPAP with continuous monitoring
  • Fluid and metabolic management
  • Surfactant administration
  • Antibiotics if sepsis suspected

😴 Apnea of Prematurity

Chapter 10

📌 Definition

  • Disorder of respiratory control
  • Absence of air flow for ≥20 seconds OR less if accompanied by bradycardia (HR <100/min) or cyanosis

Types of Apnea

  • Central: No airflow, no respiratory efforts
  • Obstructive: No airflow despite respiratory efforts
  • Mixed: Begins central, becomes obstructive

Aetiology

  • Prematurity, infection, metabolic abnormalities
  • Hypoxemia, anemia, hypo/hyperthermia
  • Gastroesophageal reflux
  • Upper airway malformations
  • Nasal blockage from secretions

💊 Prevention & Management

  • Start caffeine/aminophylline for GA <34 weeks or <1500g
  • Caffeine citrate: Loading 20mg/kg, maintenance 5-10mg/kg/day
  • Aminophylline: Loading 5mg/kg, maintenance 2mg/kg/dose 2-3x/day
  • Resuscitate patient first
  • Establish cause and treat
  • Commence CPAP with close monitoring
  • Continue KMC if baby is stable

Nutrition

Breastfeeding, feeding preterm infants, and nutritional requirements

🍼 Ten Steps for Successful Breastfeeding

Chapter 11

📋 The Ten Steps

  • Have a written breastfeeding policy
  • Train all health care staff in skills necessary to implement this policy
  • Inform all pregnant women about benefits and management of breastfeeding
  • Help mothers initiate breastfeeding within half-hour of birth
  • Show mothers how to breastfeed and maintain lactation even if separated from infants
  • Give newborn infants no food or drink other than breast milk, unless medically indicated
  • Practice rooming-in
  • Encourage breastfeeding on demand
  • Give no artificial teats or pacifiers to breastfeeding infants
  • Foster establishment of breastfeeding support groups and refer mothers on discharge

👶 Benefits to Newborn

  • Ideal nutrients, easily digestible
  • Promotes bonding
  • Improved behavioral and neurodevelopment
  • Protection against various diseases
  • Promotes growth of Lactobacilli
  • Promotes long-term health

👩 Benefits to Mother

  • Most economical
  • Gives confidence and self-sufficiency
  • Protects against postpartum hemorrhage
  • Family planning (child spacing)
  • Protection against breast and cervical cancers

🍼 Feeding Preterm Babies

Chapter 11

📊 Feeding Initiation & Advancement

  • Goal: Volume: 150 ml/kg/day; Calorie: 110-130 kcal/kg/day
  • Initial volume: 2ml/kg/feed
  • First feed is called priming or trophic feeding
  • Intended to facilitate maturation and keep integrity of preterm gut
  • Early priming facilitates tolerance and early achievement of full feeding
  • Initial feeding started with NG tube, OG tube, or cup
  • After full volume feeding achieved, frequency is every 2-3 hours

⚠️ Signs of Feeding Intolerance

  • Vomiting or presence of any gastric content
  • Abdominal distension (>2 cm within 24hrs)
  • Decreased or absent bowel sounds
  • Check residual if abdominal distention or girth increment >2 cm

💊 Vitamins and Minerals Supplementation

  • Preterm at risk for Iron deficiency anemia and Vitamin D deficiency
  • Iron: Elemental Iron 2-4mg/kg/day starting from 3rd-4th weeks of life
  • Vitamin D: 400 IU
  • Supplementation continued up to 1 year of life

Infection in Neonates

Bacterial sepsis, meningitis, and management of neonatal infections

🦠 Bacterial Sepsis

Chapter 12

📌 Definition

  • Clinical syndrome of bacteremia with systemic signs and symptoms of infection in the first 4 weeks of life
  • One of the major causes of mortality and morbidity
  • Meningitis present in 5-15% of early-onset cases and 20-30% of late-onset cases

Classification

  • Early Sepsis: Birth to 72 hours
  • Late Onset Sepsis: 72 hours to 30 days

Etiology (Developing Countries)

  • Klebsiella
  • Staphylococcus Aureus
  • Escherichia coli
  • Coagulase Negative Staphylococcus
  • Acinetobacter

🩺 Clinical Features (Non-specific)

  • Temperature instability
  • Tachycardia (HR >180) or bradycardia (HR <100)
  • Tachypnea (RR >60) or bradypnea (RR <30)
  • Poor perfusion
  • Abnormal color: cyanotic, pale, gray, mottled, jaundiced
  • Abnormal activity: irritability, seizures, floppiness, lethargy
  • Poor feeding, abdominal distention, vomiting, diarrhea
  • Hypo or hyperglycemia
  • Severe Jaundice

💊 Treatment of Bacterial Sepsis

Chapter 12

🏥 Early Onset Sepsis

  • Antibiotics: Ampicillin and Gentamicin
  • Duration:
    • Positive cultures: 10-14 days
    • Negative cultures, clinically well, normal CRP/ESR: stop after 48 hours
    • Negative cultures, not clinically well, abnormal CXR or elevated CRP: treat as probable sepsis for 5-7 days
    • No improvement after 48 hours: change to cefotaxime and Ampicillin

🏥 Late Onset Sepsis

  • Antibiotics: Ampicillin and Gentamicin
  • If critically sick or staphylococcal infection likely: triple antibiotics (cloxacillin, ampicillin, gentamicin)
  • If no improvement after 48 hours: change to cefotaxime and Ampicillin

🧠 Sepsis with Meningitis

  • Antibiotics same as for sepsis but with higher dose and prolonged duration
  • Gentamicin for two weeks, the rest for three weeks
  • Antibiotic choice needs discussion with consultant before starting second line

Chapter 13

📌 Definition

  • Almost 97% healthy full-term babies have biochemical hyperbilirubinemia

⚠️ Causes of Jaundice

  • Isoimmunization: RH incompatibility, ABO incompatibility
  • Infection: Bacterial, viral, protozoal
  • Sequestered blood: Subgaleal hemorrhage, cephalhematoma
  • Erythrocyte biochemical defect: G6PD deficiency
  • Polycythemia
  • Delayed feeding
  • Prematurity
  • Infant of diabetic mother
  • Breast feeding Jaundice
  • Hypothyroidism

💡 Phototherapy & Exchange Transfusion

Chapter 13

💡 Phototherapy

  • Bilirubin absorbs light maximally in blue range (420-470 nm)
  • Cover baby's eyes and put diaper with maximum body surface area exposed
  • Measure weight daily
  • Increase fluid intake by 20-30 ml/kg

⚠️ Side Effects of Phototherapy

  • Insensible water loss
  • Watery and frequent stool
  • Retinal damage
  • Erythema and increased blood flow
  • Bronze baby syndrome (with increased CB)
  • Low calcium level (in preterm)
  • Interferes with maternal-infant bonding

🔄 Double Volume Exchange Transfusion

  • Amount of blood volume exchanged = 2x blood volume of baby (85ml/kg)
  • Use fresh blood less than 5-7 days old
  • Procedure after umbilical catheterization using aseptic technique
  • Heparinize the catheter
  • Amount of blood removed at a time: 5ml to 20ml

🩸 Type of Blood for Exchange Transfusion

• Rh hemolytic disease: Give blood group compatible to baby and RH of mother
• ABO hemolytic disease: Give blood group of mother and Rh compatible to newborn
• O negative blood is most preferred

Neonatal Hypoglycemia & Hyperglycemia

Blood glucose disorders in newborns

📉 Neonatal Hypoglycemia

Chapter 14

📌 Definition

  • Blood glucose level <40 mg/dl in first 4 hours of life
  • Blood glucose <45 mg/dl after 4 hours of life

⚠️ Risk Factors

  • Preterm infants
  • Small for gestational age (SGA)
  • Large for gestational age (LGA)
  • Infants of diabetic mothers (IDM)
  • Sick infants
  • Infants on intravenous fluids
  • Intrapartum dextrose infusions for mother
  • Infants with polycythemia and hypothermia

🩺 Clinical Manifestations

  • Abnormal crying (weak or high-pitched)
  • Tremors, jitteriness, irritability, hypotonia
  • Seizures, lethargy, or coma
  • Poor feeding, vomiting
  • Grunting, tachypnea, tachycardia
  • Apnea, cyanosis
  • Hypothermia

💊 Management

  • Asymptomatic: Offer breast feeding immediately; check blood glucose 30 min after feeding
  • IV infusion indications: Blood glucose <25mg/dl, remains <40mg/dl after one feeding, infant becomes symptomatic, oral feeding contraindicated
  • Symptomatic: 2ml/kg of 10% glucose IV bolus over one minute; then 10% glucose infusion at GIR 6mg/kg/min (~90ml/kg/day)

📈 Neonatal Hyperglycemia

Chapter 14

📌 Definition

  • Whole blood glucose >125 mg/dl
  • Less frequently observed than hypoglycemia

⚠️ Causes

  • High rates of parenteral glucose infusion
  • Stress like asphyxia
  • Sepsis
  • Drugs (steroids, caffeine, aminophylline)
  • Prematurity - especially ELBW
  • Neonatal diabetes mellitus (rare)

💊 Management

  • Prevention by carefully adjusting GIR and frequent monitoring
  • Treat if dehydrated or in shock
  • If on dextrose, decrease infusion by 2mg/kg/min till minimum GIR of 4mg/kg/min or glucose concentration 5%
  • If not on infusion, put on 5% glucose at 4mg/kg/min (~60ml/kg/day)
  • Look for and treat underlying causes

Meconium Aspiration Syndrome (MAS)

Risk factors, clinical features, and management of MAS

🫁 Meconium Aspiration Syndrome

Chapter 15

📌 Introduction

  • Meconium stained amniotic fluid (MSAF) complicates approximately 10-15% of deliveries
  • 3-4% of neonates born through MSAF develop MAS
  • Air leak frequently complicates MAS

🔍 Pathophysiology

  • Usually secondary to fetal hypoxia
  • Increased peristalsis, relaxation of anal sphincters with release of meconium
  • Reflex gasping leads to aspiration of meconium into lungs
  • Four major pulmonary effects:
    • Airway obstruction
    • Surfactant dysfunction
    • Chemical pneumonitis
    • Bacterial Pneumonia

⚠️ Risk Factors

  • Prolonged Labor
  • Post-maturity
  • Maternal illness, diabetes, hypertension
  • Umbilical cord complications
  • Intrauterine growth retardation

🩺 Clinical Features

  • Meconium staining of nails, skin, umbilical cord
  • Increased AP diameter of chest
  • Tachypnea, retractions, grunting, cyanosis
  • Pneumothorax or pneumomediastinum

💊 Management

  • Vigorous infants born through MSAF do not need routine intubation
  • Use bulb syringe when secretions obstruct breathing
  • If not vigorous, bring to radiant warmer for initial steps
  • Routine intubation for tracheal suction not recommended for non-vigorous newborn
  • Oxygen is critically important
  • Antibiotics indicated (MAS and congenital pneumonia similar)
  • Treat complications (pneumothorax, PPHN, pneumonia, respiratory failure)

Neonatal Seizure

Etiology, clinical types, investigation, and management

⚡ Neonatal Seizures

Chapter 16

📌 Introduction

  • One of the few neonatal neurologic conditions requiring immediate medical attention
  • Usually brief and subtle
  • Manifestations of most neonatal problems like PNA, hypoglycemia, sepsis

📅 Causes by Age (1-4 days)

  • Hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy
  • Drug withdrawal
  • Drug toxicity
  • Intraventricular hemorrhage
  • Acute metabolic disorders: Hypocalcemia, Hypoglycemia, Hypomagnesemia, Hyponatremia/hypernatremia
  • Pyridoxine deficiency/dependency

📅 Causes by Age (4-14 days)

  • Infection
  • Metabolic disorders
  • Drug withdrawal
  • Benign neonatal convulsions
  • Kernicterus, hyperbilirubinemia
  • DEND syndrome

🔬 Clinical Seizure Subtypes

  • Subtle: Most common; tonic eye deviation, nystagmus, oro-bucco-lingual movements, pedaling, boxing, autonomic phenomena
  • Clonic: Stereotypic repetitive biphasic movements; unifocal, multifocal, or generalized; most common cause is neonatal stroke
  • Tonic: Sustained muscle contraction; generalized or focal; prognosis very poor
  • Myoclonic: Lightening fast contractions; multifocal or generalized; associated with poor long-term outcome

💊 Management of Neonatal Seizures

Chapter 16

🔬 Lab Studies

  • Blood glucose analysis
  • Serum electrolyte evaluation
  • Complete blood count with differential
  • CSF analysis and culture
  • Blood culture
  • Urine analysis and culture
  • EEG

🏥 Initial Management

  • Ensure airway: semi-prone position, clean oropharyngeal secretions
  • Ensure satisfactory breathing and circulation
  • Arrest seizures with appropriate orders

💊 Anticonvulsants

  • Phenobarbitone: Preferred initial drug; loading 20mg/kg IV; additional 5-10mg/kg until 40mg/kg; maintenance 3-5mg/kg in 2 divided doses
  • Phenytoin: Second agent; loading 20mg/kg; maintenance 4-6mg/kg daily
  • Lorazepam: 0.05mg/kg/dose IV over 2-5 minutes

⚠️ Hypoglycemia & Hypocalcemia

  • Hypoglycemia: 2ml/kg of 10% glucose IV bolus followed by maintenance
  • Hypocalcemia: 2ml/kg of 10% calcium gluconate IV over 10 minutes under strict cardiac monitoring

📋 Follow-up

• All medications except Phenobarbitone should be discontinued at discharge
• Phenobarbitone continued at maintenance dose of 3.5-5mg/kg
• Discontinuation of antiepileptic drugs before discharge generally recommended if normal exam, no recurrent seizures, non-epileptic EEG

Neonatal Hematologic Problems

Polycythemia, anemia, and blood transfusion

🩸 Polycythemia & Anemia

Chapter 17

📈 Polycythemia

  • Peripheral venous blood HCT >65%
  • Capillary blood sample higher by 10-15% than venous
  • Causes: Twin-to-twin transfusion, Placental insufficiency, Infant of DM, LGA, Dehydration, Congenital hypothyroidism, Trisomy 21/18/13
  • Management: Partial volume exchange transfusion, Increased fluid intake
  • Indications for exchange: Symptomatic with HCT >65%; Asymptomatic with HCT >70%

📉 Anemia

  • Hgb or HCT more than 2 SD below for age
  • For clinical purpose HCT <45%
  • Causes: Blood loss, Hemolytic anemia, Diminished RBC production, Anemia of prematurity
  • Clinical: Shock, Tachycardia, Pallor, Jaundice, Hepatosplenomegaly, Cardiac failure
  • Management: Blood transfusion based on clinical status and HCT levels

🩸 Indications for Blood Transfusion

  • Severe cardiopulmonary disease: HCT <40%
  • Moderate respiratory distress: HCT <30%
  • Major surgery & asymptomatic anemia: HCT <30%

Birth Trauma

Skull injuries, nerve injuries, bone injuries, and intra-abdominal injuries

🤕 Types of Birth Injuries

Chapter 18

Caput Succedaneum

  • Subcutaneous extra-periosteal fluid collection
  • Poorly defined margins, extends over midline and across suture lines
  • Resolves spontaneously over first several days

Cephalohematoma

  • Subperiosteal collection of blood
  • Confined by suture lines, cannot cross midline
  • Can result in significant hyperbilirubinemia
  • Management: Observation; incision and aspiration contraindicated

Subgaleal Hemorrhage

  • Hemorrhage under aponeurosis of scalp
  • Can spread across entire calvarium
  • Presentation: Pallor, poor tone, fluctuant swelling crossing suture lines
  • May grow slowly or rapidly, resulting in shock
  • Management: Admit, assess and treat shock, daily HC measurement and HCT follow-up

🧠 Brachial Plexus Injury

  • Cause: Excessive traction on head, neck, and arm during birth
  • Risk factors: Macrosomia, shoulder dystocia, breech presentation
  • Duchenne-Erb's palsy: Upper trunks (C5, C6); "waiter's tip" posture; Moro absent on affected side
  • Klumpke's palsy: C7/C8 to T1; grasp reflex absent; sensory impairment on ulnar side
  • Management: Physical therapy and passive range of motion exercises; splinting avoided

Shock

Classification, manifestations, and management of neonatal shock

⚡ Neonatal Shock

Chapter 20

📌 Definition

  • Physiologic state characterized by significant reduction of systemic tissue perfusion
  • Results in decreased tissue oxygen and nutrient delivery

Classification

  • Hypovolemic: Commonest cause; follows volume loss
  • Distributive
  • Cardiogenic
  • Obstructive

Manifestations

  • Compensated: Tachycardia, normal BP, increased SVR (cold, pale skin, oliguria, ileus)
  • Decompensated: Decreased systolic BP, lethargy, irreversible organ damage, intracranial hemorrhage

💊 Management

  • Supportive: ABC of life, correction of hypoglycemia and acidosis, intranasal oxygenation
  • Hypovolemic shock: Crystalloids; bolus of 20ml/kg over 10-20 minutes (normal saline); can go as high as 60ml/kg
  • Best volume expander: Whole O-negative blood cross-matched against mother's blood
  • Medicines: Inotropes (Dopamine, Dobutamine), Epinephrine, Phosphodiesterase inhibitors (Milrinone)

Congenital Malformations

CNS, respiratory, gastrointestinal, renal, and anogenital malformations

🧬 Neural Tube Defects (NTDs)

Chapter 21

📌 Overview

  • Common congenital anomalies of the central nervous system
  • Exact cause not known; hyperthermia, drugs, malnutrition, maternal obesity/diabetes, radiation exposure incriminated
  • Major NTDs: Spina bifida occulta, meningocele, myelomeningocele, encephalocele, anencephaly

Spina Bifida Occulta

  • Midline defect of vertebral bodies without protrusion
  • Usually in lumbar and sacral regions
  • May be covered with patches of hair, lipoma, or discoloration

Meningocele

  • Midline sac filled with CSF, mostly covered by skin
  • Leaking CSF or thin skin covering needs urgent referral

Myelomeningocele

  • Most severe form
  • Protrusion of spinal cord and meninges
  • Saclike cystic structure covered by thin membrane
  • May have flaccid paralysis, bowel/bladder incontinence
  • Associated with clubfeet and hydrocephalus

💊 Management of Myelomeningocele

  • Cover defect with sterile saline-soaked dressing
  • Prevent hypothermia
  • Place newborn in prone or lateral position
  • Antibiotics for meningitis
  • Inform parents about condition
  • Refer or consult neurosurgical side

🧬 Trisomies

Chapter 21

📌 Overview

  • Major numerical disorders of chromosomes
  • Presence of 3 instead of normal 2 chromosomes
  • Major trisomies: Trisomy 21 (Down syndrome), Trisomy 18 (Edwards syndrome), Trisomy 13 (Patau syndrome)

👶 Down Syndrome (DS)

  • Most common chromosomal abnormality among live born infants
  • Occurrence increases with advanced maternal age (≥35 yr)
  • Neonatal features: Flat face, slanted palpebral fissures, low set ears, protruded tongue, small chin, short neck, flat occiput, hypotonia, poor Moro reflex, transverse palmar (Simian) crease
  • Diagnosis: Suspected from phenotypic features (8 or more); confirmed with karyotype
  • Management: Inform and counsel parents; treat associated problems; refer for detailed evaluation

Pierre Robin Syndrome

  • Micrognathia, cleft soft palate, upper airway obstruction
  • Management: Prone with head down; endotracheal tube through nostril if needed

Choanal Atresia

  • Septum between nose and pharynx
  • Diagnosis: Difficulty breathing worsening while feeding, improves on crying
  • Management: Oropharyngeal airway; feed with orogastric tube; surgical consultation

Common Neonatal Surgical Conditions

Intestinal obstruction, abdominal wall defects, and perioperative care

🏥 Common Surgical Conditions

Chapter 22

Esophageal Atresia & TEF

  • Esophagus ends blindly with/without fistula with trachea
  • Diagnosis: Failure to pass NG tube 10-12 cms; Chest X-ray with coiled tube
  • Management: Upright position, frequent suctioning, NPO, maintenance fluid, surgical referral

Congenital Diaphragmatic Hernia

  • Failure of diaphragmatic development
  • Diagnosis: Respiratory distress, scaphoid abdomen, bowel pattern in chest on X-ray
  • Management: Resuscitation without mask ventilation, oxygen, intubation, NG tube, urgent surgical consultation

Duodenal Atresia

  • Recanalization failure of duodenum
  • Diagnosis: Early bilious vomiting, "double bubble" on X-ray
  • Management: IV line, NPO, NGT, surgical consultation

Meconium Ileus

  • Intestinal obstruction of small intestine
  • Diagnosis: Abdominal distension, bilious vomiting, failure to pass meconium, soap-bubble appearance on X-ray
  • Management: IV line, NPO, NGT, urgent consultation

💊 Preoperative Care

  • Comprehensive history and physical examination
  • Focus on airway, respiratory, cardiac, temperature, blood sugar, renal abnormalities
  • Lab studies: CBC, basic metabolic panel, coagulation studies, cross match
  • Assessment for dehydration, shock, infection, congenital malformations
  • Monitoring of vital signs, fluid input/output, pain assessment

Management of Pain in Neonates

Pain assessment, prevention, and management

😢 Pain Assessment & Management

Chapter 23

📌 Introduction

  • Preterm and term newborns demonstrate similar or exaggerated physiological and hormonal responses to pain
  • Have less ability to demonstrate pain symptoms
  • Exposure to prolonged or severe pain increases mortality and affects long-term neurodevelopmental outcome

Behavioral Indicators

  • Facial expression
  • Body movement
  • Crying

Physiological Indicators

  • Change in heart rate, respiratory rate, blood pressure
  • Oxygen saturation
  • Vagal tone
  • Palmar sweating
  • Plasma cortisol or catecholamine level

📊 Pain Assessment Tool (PAT) Score Interpretation

  • <5: Nursing Comfort Measures (NCM)
  • >5: Paracetamol and NCM
  • >10: Paracetamol, NCM, and opioid
  • PAT scores should be done at least once and minimum 4 hourly for those on ventilator
  • Sedation may mask neonate's response to painful stimuli and does not provide pain relief

💊 Pain Prevention & Management

  • Environmental/behavioral: Clustering painful interventions, Swaddling, Non-nutritive sucking, Reducing noise/light, Touch/massage, Skin-to-skin contact
  • Glucose analgesia: 25-30% glucose 1.5-3ml PO 2 min prior to procedure for term; 0.5-1.5 ml for preterm
  • Pharmacologic: Acetaminophen 10-15mg/kg; Fentanyl; Morphine (drug of choice for pain relief)

Infection Prevention

Hand hygiene, protective clothing, and instrument processing

🧼 Infection Prevention & Control

Chapter 24

📌 Definition

  • Measures a healthcare facility undertakes to prevent harm caused by infection to patients and health workers
  • Placing barriers between susceptible hosts and microorganisms
  • Most common and important route of transmission is via the hands

🧼 Hand Hygiene Practices

  • Hand washing: Mechanically remove soil and debris; 40-60 seconds with soap and water
  • Alcohol-based hand rub: 70-90% alcohol; 20-30 seconds; more effective than soap and water
  • Hand antisepsis
  • Surgical hand scrub

💡 Key Fact

Newborn survival rates increase by 44% when hand washing is in place.

🧤 Gloves Usage

  • Sterile/high-level disinfected: Contact with broken skin or invasive procedures
  • Clean examination: Examination, caring for newborns, contact with mucous membranes
  • Heavy rubber/latex: Handling contaminated items, cleaning instruments, disposing waste
  • NB: Gloves do not replace hand hygiene

🧪 Standard Solutions

  • Antiseptics: 2.5% povidone iodine, 4% chlorhexidine gluconate, 60-90% ethyl/isopropyl alcohol
  • Disinfectants: 0.5% chloride bleach, 2% glutaraldehyde

🔬 High-Level Disinfection Steps

  • Step 1: Thoroughly clean and dry instruments
  • Step 2: Completely immerse in high-level disinfectant for 20 minutes
  • Step 3: Pick items using high-level disinfected or sterile forceps/gloves
  • Step 4: Rinse well with boiled and filtered water three times, air dry
  • Step 5: Use promptly or store in dry, high-level disinfected, covered container

Common Neonatal Procedures

Oxygen administration, CPAP, blood sampling, IV line, NG tube, lumbar puncture

🩺 Common Neonatal Procedures

Chapter 25

💨 Oxygen Administration Methods

  • Nasal Prongs
  • Nasal Catheter
  • Head Box
  • Face Mask

  • Recently delivered preterm infant with minimal respiratory distress

Admission, Discharge & Follow-up

Criteria for NICU admission, discharge, and post-discharge follow-up

🏥 Admission & Discharge Criteria

Chapter 26

📋 Admission Criteria

  • Birth weight <1500g
  • Gestational age <34 weeks
  • Hematologic problems: Hyperbilirubinemia, Blood group/RH incompatibility, Anemia, Polycythemia, Bleeding disorders
  • Infection: Suspected or confirmed
  • Respiratory problems: Apnea, Cyanotic episodes, Any respiratory distress, Requirement for ventilation
  • GI problems: Feeding problems, Bile stained vomiting, Signs of bowel obstruction
  • Metabolic problems: Hypo/hyperglycemia, Hypocalcemia/hypercalcemia, Hypo/hypernatremia

  • Cardiovascular problems requiring monitoring/intervention
  • Birth trauma: Subgaleal hemorrhage, Cephalohematoma, Bone fracture

✅ Discharge Criteria

  • No danger signs or signs of serious infection
  • Low birth weight infants gaining weight (15-20gm/kg/d)
  • Ability to take all feeding by cup or breast without respiratory compromise

  • Maintains temperature in normal range (36-37°C) in open cot
  • Mother is confident and able to care for infant
  • Parents willing and comfortable to take baby

📅 Neonatal Follow-up for NICU Discharges

  • Very low birth weight babies
  • Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease
  • Asphyxia (HIE stage II and III)
  • NEC - look for nutritional status, short bowel syndrome, intestinal stricture
  • Bronchopulmonary Dysplasia (BPD)
  • Neurologic abnormalities (Hydrocephalus, Hyperbilirubinemia, Microcephaly, Neonatal seizure, Meningitis, IVH)
  • Infants requiring special equipment (Oxygen, NG-tube feeding)
  • Patients who had surgical intervention

Parental Counseling in the NICU

Communication skills, breaking bad news, and postpartum depression

👨‍👩‍👧 Parental Counseling

Chapter 27

📌 Introduction

  • In neonatology, the patient is the infant, but the parent is the recipient of information
  • Information and predictions change rapidly; decisions are complex
  • Information should be provided on an ongoing basis
  • Minimize pain and discomfort even if the baby is going to die
  • Be respectful and involve parents in care
  • Listen to family's concern and respond using simple, clear language
  • Respect privacy, confidentiality, cultural beliefs, and customs
  • Obtain informed consent before procedures, if possible

💔 When Baby is Dying or Has Died

  • Allow family to be with baby during procedure
  • If death is inevitable, focus on providing emotional support
  • Encourage family to see and hold baby after death
  • Arrange to see family after death
  • Counsel on autopsy when required

😢 Postpartum Blues

  • Transient condition characterized by mild mood swings, sadness, irritability, anxiety, decreased concentration, insomnia, tearfulness, crying spells
  • 40-80% within 2-3 days of delivery, peaks on 5th day, lasts 2 weeks
  • Common in primiparous women
  • Interventions: Support and reassurance, adequate time for sleep and rest

😔 Postpartum Depression (PPD)

  • Risk factors: Marital conflict, stressful life events, lack of support, unplanned pregnancy, previous miscarriage, family psychiatric history, not breastfeeding, unemployment, congenital malformation
  • Clinical: Changes in somatic functions, anxiety, guilt, feelings of inadequacy, not bonding to baby
  • Management: Psychosocial therapy for mild-moderate; pharmacotherapy (Benzodiazepine, Sertraline) with psychosocial support for severe

Quality Improvement in NICU

Data management, QI processes, and linkage of NICU services

📈 Quality Improvement Process

Chapter 28

🔄 The Six Steps of Quality Improvement

  • Step 1: Create an improvement team
  • Step 2: Decide what to improve
  • Step 3: Choose the barriers to overcome
  • Step 4: Plan and test change
  • Step 5: Determine if the change resulted in improvement
  • Step 6: Make improvement the norm

📊 PDSA Cycle

  • Plan: Aim, prediction, what/who/when/where/how, data collection
  • Do: Carry out plan, document observations, collect data
  • Study: Review documentation, analyze data, present in graphs (run charts)
  • Act: Adopt, Adapt, or Abandon

🔗 Linkage of NICU Services

  • NICU implementing hospitals are at different status
  • Create learning forum for poorly performing NICU facilities to share experiences
  • Create experience sharing forum for weakly performing NICUs to learn from best performing NICUs
  • Ensure senior pediatricians and neonatologists provide clinical mentorship and on-the-job training
  • Ensure best performing NICU providers are recognized and motivated
  • Establish NICU providers' association
